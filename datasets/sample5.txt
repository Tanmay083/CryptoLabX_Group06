Moss does not hurry, yet it eventually reclaims everything humans leave behind. Soft green carpets
 slip quietly over damp stone walls, decay-rotted fallen logs, and forgotten iron tracks, creating 
 miniature jungle ecosystems for tiny beetles and tardigrades. Up close, a single square inch of 
 forest moss resembles an ancient, dense canopy, holding onto moisture like a sponge and shielding
  the delicate forest soil from the blistering heat of midsummer days.
