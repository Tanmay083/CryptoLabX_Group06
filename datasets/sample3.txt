Baking sourdough is less about following a recipe 
and more about learning to read the quiet moods of living wild yeast. 
You start with flour and water, but the real magic happens over days as
 ambient microbes ferment the dough into a bubbly, fragrant paste. When it 
 finally hits a blistering-hot Dutch oven, the trapped steam forces a dramatic rise, producing a gol
 den-brown crust that crackles loudly as it cools on the counter—a sound bakers affectionately call "the bread singing."
